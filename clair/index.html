CLAIR by MicroCD Labs

Better evidence for canine dental triage.

CLAIR is a saliva-based adjunct in development for use alongside the oral examination. The proposed workflow combines collection, controlled processing on a centrifugal microfluidic disc, and optical readout to support triage and the owner conversation.

Discuss a CLAIR pilot See the workflow

Development boundary: CLAIR is not for sale or clinical use. Analytical performance, workflow timing, and clinical utility remain to be established for each panel.

The proposed clinical role

Support the examination. Explain the next step.

01
Evidence alongside the awake exam

The first development question is whether a quantitative saliva panel adds useful information when considering further dental assessment.

02
A clearer owner conversation

The proposed benefit is a clearer explanation of the recommendation for further assessment. Owner understanding must be tested.

03
Clinical judgment remains central

CLAIR is not a replacement for indicated dental assessment, probing, or radiographs. A low result must not delay otherwise indicated care.

Platform architecture

Collect saliva. Process on disc. Read and interpret.

The current engineering concept assigns sample preparation, reaction control, imaging, and interpretation to defined parts of one workflow.

Initial development focus

Four candidate measurements, a blood signal, and validity controls.

The first planned panel studies whether combined saliva evidence can support canine oral-health triage alongside the oral exam. Quantitative results and sample checks are a differentiation hypothesis, not established clinical performance.

Candidate dimensions

  • LDH Candidate tissue-activity signal
  • MPO Inflammatory activity
  • Phosphorus and pH Oral chemistry context
  • Occult blood Bleeding signal and potential interference
  • Sample validity Collection and process checks

These markers are candidates for product development. Panel composition, thresholds, performance, and clinical interpretation require analytical and clinical validation. Multiplexing alone does not establish disease specificity. CRP remains a separate research track.

Development plan

Three gates before broader rollout.

Clinical added value remains unproven. Study size and timing will follow feasibility, a funded protocol, and statistical planning.

01
Analytical readiness

Evaluate repeatability, interference, sample recovery, and collection and process controls. Report invalid samples.

02
Clinical added value

Compare the awake exam, panel alone, and exam plus panel against a blinded reference assessment using anesthetized probing and dental radiographs. Lock the panel and thresholds before independent validation and include realistic confounders.

03
Customer validation

Test workflow fit, owner understanding, willingness to pay, support needs, and repeat use with veterinary practices.

First clinic segment

Built around the veterinary team's workflow.

The proposed first customers are general practices with active dental services and an interest in structured pilot feedback.

Clinic participation
  • Practice owners and managers evaluate purchase and workflow fit.
  • Veterinarians and technicians evaluate collection, interpretation, and usability.
  • Dog owners and patients are the intended beneficiaries.
Proposed support model
  • Team onboarding for collection and sample validity
  • Interpretation guidance and owner-facing explanations
  • Troubleshooting, consumable replenishment, and feedback reviews

These roles and support needs are working hypotheses. The proposed commercial model is per-test consumables supplied to clinics; reader and software pricing, unit costs, and repeat demand require validation.
