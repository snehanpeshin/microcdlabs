CLAIR / Human + veterinary oral health

Two applications.
One focus on better-informed care.

CLAIR develops separate saliva-testing applications for human dental care and veterinary oral-health triage. Each asks whether additional evidence can help professionals guide timely assessment, appropriate treatment, and follow-up.

Human oral healthVeterinary oral health

Both programs are in development and unavailable for clinical use. Each requires its own intended use, assay panel, clinical evidence, and regulatory assessment.

Two development tracks

Distinct patients.
Distinct clinical questions.

Human oral health / Exploratory program

Quantitative saliva evidence alongside professional periodontal assessment.

The proposed initial setting is adult dental care. We will work with dental professionals to define the intended use, select candidate markers, and test whether the measurement adds useful evidence to standard assessment.

Application-specific assays

A shared platform
does not imply a shared test.

The human panel remains to be selected through clinician input, literature review, and analytical feasibility work. The veterinary candidate panel has its own development rationale.

Human program

Initial research focuses on adult periodontal assessment. Candidate selection will consider biological relevance, saliva compatibility, interference, stability, and added clinical value.

Veterinary program

Candidate signals include LDH, MPO, phosphorus, pH, occult blood, and collection and process controls. Composition and thresholds require validation.

Evidence by intended use

Separate panels.
Separate proof.

Human results cannot establish veterinary performance, and veterinary results cannot establish human performance.

If more cases are recognized, what happens next?

Recognition alone
is not the outcome.

Use this planning example to explore the care pathway. The inputs are hypothetical and are not measured CLAIR effects. “Earlier recognition” means previously unrecognized disease confirmed against a prespecified professional reference assessment.

A 10 percentage-point increase, such as 20% to 30% of a cohort, differs from a 10% relative increase. This example assumes rates can be achieved within the selected cohort.

Clinical benefit and financial savings cannot be calculated from recognition alone. False positives, missed disease, invalid tests, care completion, harms, and costs must also be measured.
