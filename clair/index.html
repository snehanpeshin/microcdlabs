CLAIR by MicroCD Labs

Making saliva more reliable for quantitative point-of-care research.

CLAIR is an in-development centrifugal microfluidic platform designed to standardize sample preparation, fluid handling, assay timing, and smartphone optical readout in one cartridge-led workflow.

Discuss a CLAIR pilot See the workflow

Development boundary: CLAIR is not for sale or clinical use. Analytical performance, workflow timing, and clinical utility remain to be established for each panel.

The development problem

Saliva is easy to collect. Reliable measurement is harder.

01
Variable sample biology

Dilution, debris, viscosity, and collection conditions can change the sample before measurement begins.

02
Manual preparation

Pipetting, timing, mixing, and transfer steps introduce operator-dependent variability.

03
Uncontrolled readout

Phone position, illumination, focus, and endpoint timing can make quantitative interpretation less reproducible.

One controlled system

The disc prepares. The reader standardizes. The assay informs.

CLAIR is being engineered as a hardware-light system in which each component has a defined role.

01

Disposable assay disc

Designed to meter the sample, support sedimentation or separation, mix reagents, and control reaction timing inside a single-use centrifugal cartridge.

02

Reusable optical reader

Designed to position a customer smartphone with fixed illumination and optics for time-resolved and endpoint image acquisition.

03

Panel-specific chemistry

Pairs the fluidic workflow with candidate markers selected for a defined veterinary decision and validated panel by panel.

Platform architecture

A cartridge-led path from collection to structured readout.

The current engineering concept assigns sample preparation, reaction control, imaging, and interpretation to defined parts of one workflow.

Intended workflow

From oral swab to structured readout.

  1. 1
    Collect

    Obtain a saliva sample using an oral swab.

  2. 2
    Load

    Transfer the sample into a panel-specific disposable disc.

  3. 3
    Process

    Use controlled rotational steps to support metering, separation, mixing, and reaction.

  4. 4
    Read

    Acquire standardized smartphone images for kinetic and endpoint analysis.

Disc operations

Control the steps that commonly add assay variability.

The disc concept organizes metering, separation, mixing, controlled imaging, and time-resolved analysis into a repeatable sequence.

Initial development focus

Canine oral-health decision support research.

The first planned panel is intended to study the clinical gray zone between routine monitoring and escalation to a more comprehensive dental workup.

Candidate panel dimensions

  • LDHTissue activity or periodontal injury
  • MPOInflammatory activity
  • Phosphorus and pHOral chemistry context
  • Occult bloodGingival bleeding signal

These markers are candidates for product development. Panel composition, thresholds, performance, and clinical interpretation require analytical and clinical validation.

Evidence discipline

Platform foundation first. Product-specific proof next.

MicroCD Labs is translating lab-on-disc, assay, imaging, and kinetic-analysis experience into a reproducible veterinary product workflow.

Foundation informing CLAIR
  • Lab-on-disc and centrifugal microfluidic development experience
  • Assay-development and time-resolved signal-analysis research
  • Prototype, motor-control, optical-reader, and cartridge engineering work
  • Patent applications and peer-reviewed technical work
Evidence still required
  • Panel-specific analytical sensitivity, precision, and interference studies
  • Transfer repeatability across discs, readers, operators, and sites
  • Veterinary workflow validation and multi-clinic pilot evidence
  • Manufacturing process, quality-control, regulatory, and commercialization planning
