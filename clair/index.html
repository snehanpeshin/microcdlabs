Platform design / Engineering objectives

Control the process. Study the signal.

  1. 01

    Collection

    Define saliva collection and sample-validity checks.

  2. 02

    Sample handling

    Develop controlled centrifugal processing on a disposable disc.

  3. 03

    Assay reaction

    Evaluate reagents, controls, and reaction conditions.

  4. 04

    Optical acquisition

    Develop controlled illumination and image capture.

  5. 05

    Analysis

    Study endpoint and time-dependent signals against reference methods.

Proposed workflow · Functional diagram, not final cartridge geometry, hardware, timing, or performance.

Why control processing, imaging, and signal analysis?

Controlled processing

Develop defined fluid handling and reaction conditions to investigate repeatability and sample validity.

Controlled imaging

Study illumination, positioning, background correction, and optical acquisition as part of the measurement method.

Kinetic analysis

Compare signal trajectories with endpoint measurements. Any added value must be measured for the specific assay.

Reader and smartphone architecture: what is established?

The platform concept uses a centrifugal disc and controlled optical readout. Existing illustrations show a reader and smartphone workflow. The linked kinetic-analysis preprint explores smartphone-compatible acquisition; it does not establish a final CLAIR reader, camera, app, or cloud implementation.

Final hardware, acquisition methods, and software integration remain in development.

If more cases are recognized, what happens next?

Recognition alone
is not the outcome.

Use this planning example to explore the care pathway. The inputs are hypothetical and are not measured CLAIR effects. “Earlier recognition” means previously unrecognized disease confirmed against a prespecified professional reference assessment.

A 10 percentage-point increase, such as 20% to 30% of a cohort, differs from a 10% relative increase. This example assumes rates can be achieved within the selected cohort.

Clinical benefit and financial savings cannot be calculated from recognition alone. False positives, missed disease, invalid tests, care completion, harms, and costs must also be measured.

100 additional recognized cases, 70 starting appropriate care, and 42 completing follow-up.

Expected counts from assumptions, not forecasts. The human and veterinary cohorts must be modeled separately.
