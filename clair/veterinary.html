The problem / Definitions matter

How many dogs
have periodontitis?

There is no single universal percentage. Age, population, examination method, and disease definition change the estimate.

59.6%

Periodontitis in one examined sample

A 2026 study of 369 anesthetized dogs over one year old reported PD2–PD4 in 59.6%. It used examination and probing without radiographs. This is a sample result, not a population-wide estimate.

Stepaniuk et al., 2026

Gingivitis ≠ periodontitis

The same study reported 98.6% for PD1–PD4 combined. PD1 is gingivitis without attachment loss. The often-quoted “80%” oral-disease figure should not be presented as a universal periodontitis rate.

12.52%

Recorded periodontal disease in UK primary care

A separate study of 22,333 dogs found a 12.52% one-year recorded diagnosis rate in 2016. Different definitions and ascertainment prevent direct comparison or subtraction to estimate missed cases.

Wallis et al., 2021

Recognition has value when it changes care

Assessment. Treatment.
Home care and reassessment.

The veterinarian determines the action after professional assessment. A saliva result alone cannot stage disease or select a procedure.

Tooth-level AVDC stages and examples of clinician-directed care
StageClinical distinctionPossible next care
PD1Gingivitis, no attachment lossProfessional cleaning and daily home plaque control
PD2Early periodontitis, <25% attachment lossProfessional periodontal care, including subgingival treatment where indicated
PD325–50% attachment lossPeriodontal therapy, specialist referral, or extraction as indicated
PD4>50% attachment lossExtraction or selected advanced periodontal treatment
Stages apply to individual teeth. Comprehensive assessment uses anesthetized probing and intraoral radiographs. Gingivitis can resolve with care; established attachment loss is not routinely reversed. Treatment aims to control disease and protect comfort and remaining teeth.

Clinical context: AVDC nomenclature, AVDC owner resources, AAHA staging and care. These resources do not endorse CLAIR.

Who benefits, and how?

The dog, the owner,
and the clinical team.

Any benefit from an additional test depends on accurate results, completed assessment, appropriate treatment, and ongoing care.

The dog / Intended beneficiary

Timely care for painful disease.

Treatment of confirmed disease can address painful pathology. CLAIR would need to demonstrate that its use helps care happen sooner and improves welfare outcomes.

Measure pain-related function, disease progression, and tooth retention.

The owner / Care decision-maker

A clearer decision and care plan.

Additional evidence may support understanding and follow-through. Early care could reduce later complexity, but tests and treatment add costs now. Lifetime bill savings are unproven.

Measure understanding, adherence, time burden, and total costs over follow-up.

The veterinary team / Professional user

Evidence for appropriate recommendations.

The value is better clinical decisions and communication, not more procedures by default. A low test result must not delay already indicated care.

Measure decision quality, treatment completion, workflow time, and unnecessary referrals.

01 / Intended role

Support the examination.
Explain the next step.

A saliva-based adjunct for the conversation about further dental assessment.

CLAIR asks whether a quantitative saliva panel can add useful evidence alongside the awake oral exam. The first planned setting is general veterinary practices with active dental services.

Clinical judgment remains central. CLAIR does not replace indicated dental assessment, probing, or radiographs. A low result must not delay otherwise indicated care.

04 / Evidence before rollout

Three questions.
Three development gates.

Clinical added value remains unproven. Study size and timing depend on feasibility, a funded protocol, and statistical planning.

  1. 01

    Analytical readiness

    Can the panel measure reliably?

    Evaluate repeatability, interference, sample recovery, and collection and process controls. Report invalid samples.

  2. 02

    Independent clinical evaluation

    Does it add useful evidence?

    Compare the awake exam, panel alone, and exam plus panel against a blinded reference assessment using anesthetized probing and dental radiographs. Lock the panel and thresholds before independent validation and include realistic confounders.

  3. 03

    Customer validation

    Does it work for the clinic?

    Study appropriate assessment and treatment completion, owner understanding, support needs, pain-related outcomes, follow-up adherence, and total costs. Quantify false positives, false negatives, and invalid samples.
