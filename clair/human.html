Clinical background, care context & references

Human disease burden

A substantial need.
A specific research question.

NIDCR reports 42.2% periodontitis among dentate U.S. adults aged 30 and older in NHANES 2009–2014.

This historical population estimate is not the prevalence of a proposed clinic cohort and does not establish demand for CLAIR.

NIDCR prevalence data

Recognition to care

A dental professional selects the next action.

Clinical assessment may lead to professional cleaning, scaling and root planing, risk-factor management, maintenance, or specialist care. Treatment depends on the findings. Gingivitis may resolve, while established periodontitis requires disease control and ongoing care.

NIDCR assessment and treatment

Human diagnosis and staging use human clinical criteria. AVDC stages apply to veterinary dentistry and are not used for the human program.

Who benefits?

Patients and their
dental care teams.

The patient

Appropriate care and understanding.

The intended benefit is timely treatment and support for oral comfort and tooth retention. CLAIR-specific clinical gains need prospective evidence.

Measure treatment completion, patient-reported outcomes, and periodontal change.

The dental team

Evidence for a clinical decision.

Test whether results add useful information beyond examination, probing, history, and indicated imaging. Avoid unnecessary escalation and false reassurance.

Measure incremental value, workflow time, invalid results, and decision quality.

The payer or practice

An economic case to establish.

Assess test costs, additional visits, treatment, and longitudinal outcomes. Reimbursement, purchasing intent, and net savings remain open questions.

Measure net cost and willingness to pay. Avoid assumed savings.

The proposed clinical question

What can saliva add
to the assessment?

The starting point is a defined question in professional adult dental care.

Dental professionals use clinical examination, periodontal probing, patient history, and radiographs when indicated. The human CLAIR program will investigate whether a quantitative saliva measurement adds useful information alongside that assessment.

A saliva result would not replace examination, probing, or indicated imaging. The initial concept does not claim to diagnose caries, oral cancer, or systemic disease.

Proposed professional workflow

A defined sample.
A controlled measurement.

  1. 01 / Collect

    Standardize collection

    Define collection timing, oral hygiene and food restrictions, storage, and sample-validity checks in the study protocol.

  2. 02 / Measure

    Process and read

    Develop the centrifugal assay disc and controlled optical readout around the selected human panel.

  3. 03 / Evaluate

    Compare with assessment

    Assess results against a prespecified periodontal reference assessment and intended clinical role.

Human evidence plan

A human study plan
for a human claim.

Timing, sample size, and performance targets require clinical, statistical, ethics, and regulatory input.

  1. 01

    Definition and feasibility

    Lock the intended question

    Define adult population, dental setting, candidate markers, sample collection, comparator, and the decision a result could support. Study the human saliva matrix.

  2. 02

    Clinical evaluation

    Test added value independently

    Use a prespecified protocol and appropriate ethics review and consent. Address confounders, blinded reference assessment, invalid results, and independent validation after locking the panel and thresholds.

  3. 03

    Product and workflow

    Establish the route to use

    Evaluate professional usability, sample traceability, quality controls, manufacturing, and the appropriate regulatory pathway for the final intended use. No clearance or route is assumed.
