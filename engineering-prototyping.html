The engineering question

A work product tied to your next decision.

A prototype is useful when it answers a question. Before creating detailed geometry, define the interfaces it must represent, the loads or operating conditions it will encounter, and the result the team needs to inspect. That keeps modeling and fabrication effort tied to the project decision.

Example work products

  • Concept CAD and interface drawings within an agreed model and drawing scope.
  • Fixture requirements or mechanical layouts for a defined bench task.
  • A build-and-test plan with material and fabrication assumptions.
  • A technical handoff package containing the agreed files, vendor questions, and open issues.

These are examples, not a bundled commitment. The agreed scope defines the actual outputs and review criteria.

Useful starting inputs

  • Existing sketches, models, drawings, or photographs that you are permitted to share.
  • Critical interfaces, approximate envelope, operating conditions, and known constraints.
  • The proposed fabrication route or supplier requirements, if already chosen.
  • The test or decision the prototype should support and the required handoff format.

Share non-confidential information in the initial enquiry. A finished specification is not required.

Decisions to make explicit

Questions the work can help resolve.

Interfaces and fit

Which dimensions, alignment features, connections, and service access must be represented in the prototype?

Build route

What can the chosen fabrication process demonstrate, and which properties will still need a later build or test?

Test and handoff

Before we begin

Common project questions.

Can we request a small, defined piece of CAD work?

Yes. The engagement can be scoped around a model, interface drawing, fixture brief, or technical review. Inputs, revisions, outputs, and review criteria should be agreed first.

Does prototyping support mean in-house manufacturing?

No. Fabrication routes and suppliers are defined per project. In-house manufacturing, certified production, or supplier availability are not implied.

Can an existing design be reviewed?

Yes. A review can focus on interfaces, assumptions, missing requirements, or the questions a prototype should resolve. Production readiness requires the evidence and controls appropriate to the intended application.
