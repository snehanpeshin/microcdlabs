The engineering question

A work product tied to your next decision.

An application may be clear while the route through a device is not. Sample preparation, fluid sequencing, interfaces, materials, and the measurement step need to work together. Consulting can help separate the decisions that can be made from existing evidence from the questions that require an experiment.

Example work products

  • A requirements and constraints brief covering volumes, fluids, interfaces, and the intended workflow.
  • A functional block diagram or sample-to-readout workflow with stated assumptions.
  • A comparison of candidate architectures, components, or material routes against agreed criteria.
  • A prototype and experiment roadmap identifying the next uncertainty to resolve.

These are examples, not a bundled commitment. The agreed scope defines the actual outputs and review criteria.

Useful starting inputs

  • A non-confidential description of the application and current assay steps.
  • Known sample and reagent volumes, fluid properties, and compatibility constraints.
  • Existing sketches, bench observations, or test data, if available.
  • The next decision, development stage, and desired deliverable.

Share non-confidential information in the initial enquiry. A finished specification is not required.

Fluid sequencing

Before we begin

Common project questions.

Can we begin before the device requirements are complete?

Yes. A focused requirements or feasibility brief can be the initial work product. Open questions and assumptions should remain explicit.

Does consulting include chip fabrication?

Fabrication is not automatically included. Any prototype route, supplier role, build responsibility, and acceptance criteria must be agreed in the project scope.

Can you help with lab-on-disc workflows?

Lab-on-disc and centrifugal workflows are within the technical focus. The scope can address sequence maps, interfaces, and prototype planning; performance must be established through testing.
